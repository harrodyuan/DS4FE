Subject: Re: Sample TAQ Data — Initial Findings and Feature Engineering Directions

Hi Chad,

Thanks for the pointer to the NYSE TAQ samples. I downloaded and inspected the data and have a first pass at feature engineering. Here is a concise summary of what I found and what I think we can do next.

1. What is in the sample directory

The FTP site contains a wide range of sample feeds:

- DAILY TAQ: consolidated NBBO (~9.5 GB/day), consolidated trades (~3.2 GB/day), reference master, and split BBO files by ticker letter.
- Venue-specific: NYSE BBO, trades, integrated feed, order imbalances, and OpenBook depth.
- Other venues: NYSE Arca, American, National, etc.

I downloaded four files: the reference master, the NYSE BBO first hour, the NYSE trades, and the consolidated full-day trades. The full-day consolidated trades file is the most useful for trade-flow features.

2. Schema and parsing

I reverse-engineered the formats from the raw files:

- Reference master: pipe-delimited, 40 fields, 12,181 symbols, with per-venue trading flags.
- NYSE BBO quotes: comma-delimited, msg type 140, field order [ask_px, ask_sz, bid_px, bid_sz]. Quote condition 4 = one-sided update, 5 = two-sided quote.
- Consolidated trades: pipe-delimited, fields include timestamp, exchange, symbol, sale condition, volume, and price.

All parsing scripts are checked into the repo under research/.

3. Feature engineering built

I built a 1-minute bar engine and computed:

- Quote-based: mean/median/max spread (bps), quote intensity, depth imbalance, log quoted depth, rolling volatility.
- Trade-based: total volume, trade count, VWAP, average trade size, dollar volume, sale-condition breakdown.
- Cross-sectional: full-day volume/volatility profiles for SPY, QQQ, TSLA, AAPL, IWM, MSFT.

4. Key empirical findings

- ETFs (SPY, QQQ, IWM) dominate by volume and trade in large blocks. Single-name tech stocks (TSLA, MSFT) have many small trades.
- AAPL saw 48.8M shares and 642K trades on 2026-01-02; SPY saw 93.5M shares and 1.25M trades.
- BBO quote frequency in the first hour reaches 10–90 quotes per second for the most liquid names.
- Tightest spreads in the opening hour are on ETFs (~10–40 bps); single-name spreads are wider.
- Simple 1-minute predictive models (Ridge for volatility, Logistic for direction) are noise-level on one hour of data. This is expected — high-frequency prediction needs much more data.

5. Best directions for feature engineering

Given your focus on feature engineering, I see the most promising projects as:

A. Link TAQ equity microstructure to the existing options implied-volatility work. Use daily microstructure features (spread, volume, volatility signature) from SPY/SPX constituents to predict next-day ATM implied vol and skew changes. This directly bridges the two datasets we are already building.

B. Implement VPIN (Volume-Synchronized Probability of Informed Trading) on the consolidated trade data. This is a classic, well-cited microstructure feature that predicts short-term stress and volatility spikes.

C. Build a daily liquidity stress index across all symbols: median spread, fraction of stocks with spreads > 100 bps, Amihud illiquidity, and cross-sectional dispersion. Compare it to VIX and SPX realized volatility.

D. Auction imbalance signals from the NYSE ORDER IMBALANCES feed. Opening and closing auction imbalances are known predictors of price pressure around the open/close.

E. Full LOB depth features from NYSE OpenBook, once available. Queue pressure, depth asymmetry, and level-2+ features are stronger than top-of-book BBO alone.

6. Practical considerations

- Data volume: one full day of consolidated trades + NBBO is ~12 GB compressed. A month is ~250 GB, two years ~6 TB. We will need to decide on a sampling strategy.
- Parsing speed: Python/pandas handles ~100K messages/sec; for full production use, polars or chunked pyarrow would be 10x faster.
- The first-hour BBO sample is too short for reliable predictive modeling. A full-day or multi-day panel is needed.
